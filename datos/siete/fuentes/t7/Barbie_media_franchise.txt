'''Barbie''' is an American [[media franchise|multimedia franchise]] created by [[Mattel]] based on [[barbie|the eponymous fashion doll]] created by its co-founder, [[Ruth Handler]]. It began with the release of [[barbie (1984 video game)|an eponymous]] [[video game]] in 1984 and has since been featured in [[list of Barbie video games|more video games]], a [[film series]] and in other media formats across technologies like television and the Internet.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie in pop culture|url=http://www.barbiemedia.com/pop-culture/entertainment.html|website=[[Barbie|Barbie Media]]|access-date=27 March 2022}}</ref> It is one of the [[List of highest-grossing media franchises|highest-grossing media franchises]] of all time and has been referred to among fans as the "Barbie Cinematic Universe".<ref>{{Cite web|last=Laurie|first=Virginia|date=22 January 2022|title=The Legacy of the Barbie Cinematic Universe|url=https://studybreaks.com/tvfilm/barbie-cinematic-universe/|website=Study Breaks|access-date=5 July 2022}}</ref>

The film series were released [[direct-to-video]] from 2001 and aired regularly on [[Nickelodeon]] as [[television special]]s in the United States from 2002, with both running until early 2017. Since mid-2017, beginning with ''[[Barbie Dolphin Magic]]'', Mattel revamped them into [[streaming television|streaming]] [[television film]]s, branded or marketed them as animated "specials" with a reduced 1-hour video length as opposed to the feature films beforehand and moved them to [[streaming media]] services, [[Netflix]], but also including [[Amazon Prime Video]], [[Google Play]] and [[Apple TV+]]. These films released from late 2017 onwards could still be released on [[home video]] formats, locally, or get picked up for television broadcast in multiple countries and regions/territories. Aside from the film series, Barbie has been featured in other media formats, including two animated direct-to-video specials in ''[[Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World]]'' and its sequel, ''[[Barbie and the Sensations: Rockin' Back to Earth]]'', both in 1987, [[web series]] like ''[[Barbie: Life in the Dreamhouse]]'', ''Barbie: Vlogger'', ''[[Barbie Dreamtopia]]'', ''Barbie's Dreamworld'' and ''Barbie: Life in the City'', [[television show]]s like ''[[Barbie Dreamhouse Adventures]]'', ''[[Barbie: It Takes Two]]'' and  ''[[Barbie: A Touch of Magic]]'', [[Barbie (film)|a live-action film]] and [[Barbie: Epic Road Trip|an interactive film]].

==Origins==
Barbie debuted on March 9, 1959, at the [[North American International Toy Fair|American International Toy Fair]] (now the ''North American International Toy Fair'') and it was well received locally in the United States, sparking a revolution in the [[toy]] industry. Since then, numerous likenesses of the doll were released which later evolved into what is now considered as "[[list of Barbie's friends and family|Barbie's friends and family]]" and clothing and accessories to accompany them. Various print [[hardcover]] and comic books starring Barbie were also released through partnerships and/or deals with [[Dell Comics]], [[Marvel Comics]] and currently [[Papercutz]].<ref name="PCutz">{{Cite web |last=Bunge |first=Nicole |date=13 May 2016 |title=Papercutz Launching 'Barbie' Graphic Novel Series |url=https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/34476/papercutz-launching-barbie-graphic-novel-series |access-date=27 July 2022 |website=ICv2 |language=en}}</ref>

In the 1980s, the net popularity and sales of Barbie dolls in the toy market began to decline<ref name="Fem" /> and struggled further in 2001 when [[MGA Entertainment]] introduced a line of dolls under the name [[Bratz]], whose [[sexualized]] characters contrasted with Barbie's older, [[Chastity|chaste]] image.<ref name="Muscle">Ault, Susanne, "Girl Power Builds Barbie muscle: Mattel Takes Kids' Fave Forward with U Pact," Daily Variety (February 12, 2007). A11-A13.</ref><ref name="Converge">Orr, Lisa, "'Difference That is Actually Sameness Mass-Reproduced,: Barbie Joins the Princess Convergence," Jeunesse: Young People, Texts Cultures 1.1 (2009): 9-30.</ref> Mattel therefore attempted to overhaul the Barbie brand to keep the toy relevant to consumers in the [[modern age]] by bringing in consultants and conducting research on key market groups. Mattel consultants initially considered reducing Barbie's [[breast size]], but later claimed that the doll's figure could not be changed because "being consistent is one of her biggest strengths".<ref>"Holding Back the Years." ''Marketing Week''. Marketing Week, 14 Aug. 2008. Web. 22 Apr. 2009.</ref> Mattel's team also considered targeting market audiences of [[career women]] and their daughters by introducing lines of Doctor or Lawyer Barbies.<ref name="Trouble">Gogoi, Pallavi. "Mattel's Barbie Trouble." ''Business Week Online'' 18 July 2006: 7. ''Academic Search Premier''. Web. 15 Mar. 2009.</ref> However, Mattel's research showed that girls were spending more time online than playing with physical dolls.<ref name="Fem" /><ref name="Trouble" /><ref>Netherby, Jennifer. "Doll Shape Shifts with Times." Daily Variety 9 Feb. 2007. LexisNexis. Web. 25 Oct. 2008.</ref> Therefore, targeted career Barbies were discarded in favor of looking for more interactive platforms through online or [[digital media]].

In response, Mattel began adapting Barbie into various facets of media and entertainment beyond the [[television advertisement]] of its dolls and related accessories (which was a prolific marketing strategy in the past). For the first 16 entries in the film series, Barbie is featured as a [[virtual actor|virtual actress]] playing the main character, and often being portrayed as a modern girl telling the story to one of her sisters or a younger friend – as a parable to present affairs. Scholars examining how the Barbie films differ from other princess narratives have concluded that Mattel intentionally attempted to remediate its brand based on [[Feminist theory|feminist]] criticisms through storytelling in the films.<ref name="Fem">Vered, Karen Orrl & Maizonniaux, Christèlel, "Barbie and the straight-to-DVD movie: pink post-feminist pedagogy", Feminist Media Studies. Apr 2017, Vol. 17 Issue 2, p198-214. 17p.</ref><ref>Still, Julie, "Feminist Barbie: Mattel's Remakes of Classic Tales", MP: A Feminist Journal Online. Oct 2010, Vol. 3 Issue 2, p148-164. 17p.</ref>

==History==
In 1987, Mattel produced two [[television special]]s with [[DIC Entertainment|DIC Animation City]] and [[BVS Entertainment|Saban Productions]]; ''[[Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World]]'' and its sequel ''[[Barbie and the Sensations: Rockin' Back to Earth]]'', both featuring Barbie as the leader of a rock band (often seen as being Mattel's answer to rival fashion doll ''[[Jem (TV series)|Jem]]'' from [[Hasbro]]); Mattel had previously avoided media projects for Barbie “for fear of giving Barbie a cartoon-type personality and not leaving anything to the imagination”. Though often speculated to be potential pilots for a regular Barbie series, no series ever happened. Instead, both specials were released direct-to-video by Hi-Tops Video, the children's division of [[Media Home Entertainment]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2023-07-27 |title='Truly Outrageous': The 1980s War Between Jem and the Holograms and Barbie and the Rockers |url=https://cafe.com/article/truly-outrageous-the-1980s-war-between-jem-and-the-holograms-and-barbie-and-the-rockers/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=CAFE |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Lapin |first=Lisa a |date=1986-10-06 |title=Barbie Takes Up Rock 'n' Roll to Match Rival Jem |url=https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1986-10-06-fi-4330-story.html |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=Los Angeles Times |language=en-US}}</ref> Another planned series for the fall of 1987 by the newly formed Mattel division MTS Entertainment was, unusually, a newsmagazine aimed at kids and teenagers, intended to feature both live-action and animated segments. Due to a flooded syndication market for children, the series never progressed, beyond a presentation at the 1987 [[NATPE]] convention.<ref>{{Cite web |date=10 November 1986 |title=Syndication Marketplace |url=https://www.worldradiohistory.com/hd2/IDX-Business/Magazines/Archive-BC-IDX/86-OCR/BC-1986-11-10-OCR-Page-0075.pdf |access-date=5 February 2024 |website=World Radio History}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=19 January 1987 |title=NATPE 87 -- Bleak days for animation |url=https://www.worldradiohistory.com/hd2/IDX-Business/Magazines/Archive-BC-IDX/87-OCR/BC-1987-01-19-OCR-Page-0130.pdf |access-date=5 February 2024 |website=World Radio History}}</ref>

With the boom of [[home video]] marketing at the time, Mattel launched an eponymous in-house entertainment division in 2001<ref name="MatEnt">{{cite news |last=Schiller |first=Gail |date=2 August 2007 |title=Barbie, Hot Wheels, G.I. Joe toying with Hollywood |work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] |publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]] |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/barbie-hot-wheels-gi-joe-146206/ |access-date=2 February 2022 |quote=But it wasn't until 2001 that Mattel started to get into productions of their own, mostly with direct-to-DVD content. Mattel launched its own in-house entertainment division that year and since then has sold 40 million copies of 10 Barbie DVD and VHS titles, generating more than $700 million in revenue.}}</ref> and began the creation of the ''Barbie'' films with the [[direct-to-video]] release of ''Barbie in the Nutcracker.'' The films initially revolved around Barbie being re-imagined as a princess and eventually expanded into various worlds of fashion and fantasy. A primary benefit of this strategy revolved around marketing, as Mattel could sell dolls specific to each film separately from the video sales and merchandise related to props, costumes and sets from the films.<ref name="Fem" />

Tim Kilpin, then-senior vice-president for girls marketing at Mattel, stated: "''What you see now are several different Barbie worlds anchored by content and storytelling. A girl can understand what role Barbie is playing, what the other characters are doing, and how they interrelate. That's a much richer level of story that leads to a richer level of play.''"<ref>"Barbie's Midlife Crisis." ''Brand Strategy'' 14 May 2004: 20+. ''LexisNexis''. Web. 25 Oct. 2008.</ref> The strategy worked as U.S. Barbie sales, led by the princess line, "increased by two percent in 2006, saving Mattel's bottom line at a time when its worldwide share of the toy market was declining."<ref name="Trouble" /><ref>Mattel. "Mattel Incorporated Fourth Quarter 2008 Earnings Conference Call." ''Mattel''. Mattel, 2 Feb. 2009. Web. 16 Mar. 2009.</ref> Within the films, Mattel included performances by well-known companies and orchestras, such as the [[London Symphony Orchestra]], the [[Czech Philharmonic]] and the [[New York City Ballet]].<ref name="Fem" /><ref>{{Cite web |author=Variety Staff |date=2007-02-10 |title=Girl power builds Barbie muscle |url=https://variety.com/2007/biz/features/girl-power-builds-barbie-muscle-1117959178/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> These associations could have been included as an enrichment strategy on behalf of Mattel's marketing team to help the films be seen as educational.

Canadian animation studio [[Jam Filled Entertainment|Arc Productions, Inc.]] produced the first non-Barbie-film production for Mattel in 2011, a [[web series]] called ''[[Barbie: Life in the Dreamhouse]]'' which debuted on the official Barbie website and [[YouTube]] on May 11, 2012, and concluded on November 27, 2015. The web series later spawned two television specials which were broadcast in the U.S. on [[Nickelodeon]]. Impressed, Mattel gave them four Barbie films to produce, enlisting them as the third production partner for the films after [[Mainframe Studios]] (at the time known as "Rainmaker Entertainment") and [[Vantiva|Technicolor]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barbie celebrates 25th DVD release today |url=https://ew.com/article/2013/02/26/barbie-25-dvd-release-pink-shoes/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=EW.com |language=en}}</ref>

Israeli animation studio Snowball Studios, with support from the Jerusalem Film and Television Fund,<ref name="Jeru">{{cite web |title=Barbie™ Dreamtopia |url=https://thejerusalemfilmfund.com/projects-in-development/barbie-dreamtopia/ |access-date=2 February 2022 |website=The Jerusalem Film & Television Fund}}</ref> produced another web series, this time created by Nickelodeon alum Julia Pistor, for [[Mattel Films|Mattel Playground Productions]] known as ''[[Barbie Dreamtopia|Barbie: Dreamtopia]]''.<ref name="BSB">{{cite web |title=Barbie - Dreamtopia |url=https://www.snowballvfx.com/dreamtopia |access-date=2 February 2022 |website=Snowball Studios}}</ref><ref name="Imaginar">{{cite web |title=Snowball Barbie Dreamtopia Titles Animation |url=https://www.imaginar.co.uk/snowball-barbie-dreamtopia-titles-animation/ |access-date=2 February 2022 |website=Imaginar}}</ref> This series of 2-minute to 4-minute shorts launched on May 21, 2016, by Mattel's newest company, [[Mattel Television|Mattel Creations]], on YouTube's affiliate platform, ''[[YouTube Kids]]''. The web shorts series later spawned an eponymous [[television film]] (distributed by [[Universal Pictures Home Entertainment]] and broadcast on television in over 5 countries and regions/territories), one web-based special and a 26-episode web series – therefore becoming the first web-exclusive Barbie media franchise. This would be the catalyst for scholars and enthusiasts alike to notice Mattel heavily invested and involved in releasing more related Barbie web series and miniseries bundles on YouTube and its variants; YouTube Kids and [[YouTube Shorts]].

Through the short video bundles on YouTube, Mattel also provided a [[online video platform|platform]] for Barbie to give its audience a look into her [[Barbie#Fictional biography|fictional life]] while trying to educate them along the way. With a YouTube channel having over 11 million subscribers,<ref name="BYT">{{Cite web |title=Barbie - YouTube |url=https://www.youtube.com/c/barbie |access-date=25 July 2022 |work=[[Barbie]] |publisher=[[Mattel]] |via=[[YouTube]]}}</ref> Mattel introduced Barbie as a [[YouTuber]] through a web series called ''Barbie Vlogger'' (or ''Barbie Vlogs''), uploading its first video on June 19, 2015. Julia Pistor, who collaborated on the series as an executive producer and writer, stated: ''"Barbie is conscious of language and words; she talks about intention and she's self-reflective. While we might use words that kids sometimes need to look up, we try to be true to Barbie being a 17-year-old influencer."'' By giving a self-reflective nature to Barbie's character, this would allow her to discuss difficult topics (such as [[mental health]] and [[racism]]) in such a way that it inspires its audience to think about and discuss those topics as well.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2 September 2020|title=Fictional Barbie's real YouTube career |url=https://www.diggitmagazine.com/papers/fictional-barbies-real-youtube-career |access-date=25 July 2022|website=Diggit Magazine |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Scott |first=Ellen |date=30 May 2017 |title=Why it's so powerful for Barbie to talk about mental health |url=https://metro.co.uk/2017/05/30/why-its-so-powerful-for-barbie-to-talk-about-mental-health-6671475/|access-date=27 March 2022 |website=[[Metro (British newspaper)|Metro]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Mitchell |first=Skylar |date=10 October 2020 |title=Barbie confronts racism in viral video and shows how to be a White ally |url=https://www.cnn.com/2020/10/10/us/barbie-vlog-teaches-allyship-trnd/index.html |access-date=27 March 2022 |website=[[CNN]] |language=en}}</ref>

With the success of its online platforms, Mattel would continue to produce web series and mini-series on YouTube and its variants. Beginning with [[Barbie Dolphin Magic|''Barbie: Dolphin Magic'']] in 2017, the media franchise as a whole moved over to [[streaming media]] services, [[Netflix]], also including [[Amazon Prime Video]], [[Google Play]] and [[Apple TV+]], with Mattel still maintaining the possibility of [[linear television]] broadcast agreements. In addition, Mattel signed an agreement on May 1, 2020, with [[Arts Music|Arts Music, Inc.]], a then-newest record label of [[Warner Music Group]], to make thousands of songs from their brand portfolio – including Barbie – available through [[online shopping|online]] [[music streaming service]]s.<ref>{{cite press release |date=1 May 2020 |url=https://corporate.mattel.com/news/mattel-and-warner-music-group-announce-exclusive-global-distribution-partnership |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220206163019/https://corporate.mattel.com/news/mattel-and-warner-music-group-announce-exclusive-global-distribution-partnership |archive-date=6 February 2022 |title=Mattel and Warner Music Group Announce Exclusive Global Distribution Partnership |publisher=[[mattel|Mattel Corporate]] |access-date=4 January 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Forde |first=Eamonn |date=4 May 2020 |title=Warner Music Group signs distribution partnership with Mattel |url=https://musically.com/2020/05/04/warner-music-group-signs-distribution-partnership-with-mattel/ |access-date=2 February 2022 |website=Music Ally}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Millman |first=Ethan |date=1 May 2020 |title=Warner Music's Newest Artists Are... Hot Wheels and Barbie |url=https://www.rollingstone.com/pro/news/warner-mattel-distribution-deal-993102/ |access-date=5 February 2022 |magazine=[[Rolling Stone]]}}</ref> The agreement began taking effect a week later when the soundtrack albums tied to their related productions are made available through WMG's distribution label, [[Alternative Distribution Alliance|ADA Worldwide]]. Mattel would extend its collaboration with Netflix on October 21, 2022, 4 days before the American debut of the franchise's inaugural interactive "special", ''Barbie: Epic Road Trip'', which would also see the pre-2017 film catalogue previously held by Universal made available occasionally through the streaming service.<ref name="BNExp">{{cite web|last=Porter|first=Rick|date=21 October 2022|title=Mattel Inks Netflix Deal, Sets Barbie Interactive Special (Exclusive)|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/netflix-mattel-barbie-deal-1235245557/|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|publisher=[[Penske Media Corporation]]|access-date=21 October 2022}}</ref><ref name="MoreBarbie">{{cite web|last=Adams|first=Timothy|date=21 October 2022|title=Mattel Signs Deal With Netflix for More Barbie Programming|url=https://comicbook.com/tv-shows/news/mattel-netflix-deal-more-barbie-programming/|website=[[ComicBook.com]]|access-date=9 November 2022}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=22 October 2022 |title=Mattel, Netflix deal for Barbie interactive special |url=https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/mattel-netflix-deal-for-barbie-interactive-special-details-here/articleshow/95020423.cms |access-date=9 November 2022 |work=[[The Economic Times]]}}</ref>

==Films==
{{Further|List of Barbie films}}
After the [[broadcast syndication]] of 2 television specials in 1987, numerous video game releases in the 1990s and the rise of home video marketing, Mattel collaborated with Canadian company, [[Mainframe Studios|Mainframe Entertainment]] (currently ''Mainframe Studios'') to produce its first computer-animated feature-length film, ''[[Barbie in the Nutcracker]]'', based on [[E. T. A. Hoffmann]]'s [[The Nutcracker and the Mouse King|classic tale]] and [[Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky|Tchaikovsky]]'s [[The Nutcracker|accompanying ballet music]], for a 2001 release through [[Family Home Entertainment]]. Mainframe continues to produce the majority of films in the series. The first decade was dominated by films based on pre-existing stories/tales, including [[Brothers Grimm]]'s fairy tales: [[Rapunzel]] and [[The Twelve Dancing Princesses]], Tchaikovsky's [[Swan Lake]] ballet, [[Charles Dickens]]' [[A Christmas Carol]], [[Hans Christian Andersen]]'s [[Thumbelina]] and [[Alexandre Dumas]]' [[The Three Musketeers]].<ref>{{Cite journal|last1=Vered|first1=Karen Orr|last2=Maizonniaux|first2=Christèle|date=14 March 2020|title=Barbie and the straight-to-DVD movie: pink post-feminist pedagogy|journal=Feminist Media Studies|volume=17|issue=2|pages=198–214|doi=10.1080/14680777.2016.1178158|s2cid=147748759}}</ref>

The second film in the series, ''[[Barbie as Rapunzel]]'', saw the debut of the film series on American television via [[Nickelodeon]] as television specials. For home video formats, the release of the series on [[VHS]] ceased in 2006 after ''[[Barbie Fairytopia: Mermaidia]]'' following a general gradual decline in the use of the format and the transfer of distribution duties for the series to Universal (taking over from Lionsgate (''now'' [[Starz Entertainment]]), which had absorbed FHE and [[Artisan Entertainment]] in 2003, in part because of a breakdown in negotiations over rights fees<ref>{{Cite web |last=B |first=Brian |date=2006-03-24 |title=Lionsgate and Mattel Say No More Barbie DVDs |url=https://movieweb.com/lionsgate-and-mattel-say-no-more-barbie-dvds/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=MovieWeb |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Netherby |first=Jennifer |date=2006-04-13 |title=U enters the Barbie biz |url=https://variety.com/2006/scene/markets-festivals/u-enters-the-barbie-biz-1117941462/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Jacobs |first=Evan |date=2006-04-11 |title=Barbie Finds New Home at Universal |url=https://movieweb.com/barbie-finds-new-home-at-universal/ |access-date=2024-02-05 |website=MovieWeb |language=en}}</ref>) in favor of [[DVD-Video|DVD]] releases only. The Blu-ray and [[digital copy|digital copies]] (branded as "Digital HD") were introduced to accompany the DVD with the release of ''Barbie in the Pink Shoes'' in 2013.<ref>{{cite AV media|date=9 September 2012|title=Barbie in The Pink Shoes - Official Teaser Trailer (HD)|publisher=[[YouTube]]|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oCw7hwiqi0Y|quote=See the last screen of the video for proof.}}</ref><ref name="Signs">{{cite web|date=18 April 2015|title=Barbie shows signs of life as Mattel plots comeback|url=https://www.freep.com/story/life/2015/04/18/barbie-makeover-comeback-mattel/25981851/|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211022154344/https://www.freep.com/story/life/2015/04/18/barbie-makeover-comeback-mattel/25981851/|archive-date=22 October 2021|website=[[Detroit Free Press]]|access-date=20 October 2021}}</ref>

Due to the popularity of the 2004 film ''[[Barbie as the Princess and the Pauper]]'' (based on [[Mark Twain]]'s [[The Prince and the Pauper|famous novel]]), a [[remake]] was released in 2012 entitled ''[[Barbie: The Princess & the Popstar]]'',<ref>{{Cite journal|last=Orr|first=Lisa|date=15 March 2020|title="Difference That Is Actually Sameness Mass-Reproduced": Barbie Joins the Princess Convergence|url=https://muse.jhu.edu/article/406626|journal=Jeunesse: Young People, Texts, Cultures|volume=1|issue=1|pages=9–30|doi=10.3138/jeunesse.1.1.9 |via=Project MUSE|access-date=15 March 2020|archive-date=2 June 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180602192206/http://muse.jhu.edu/article/406626|url-status=live|url-access=subscription}}</ref> which itself inspired two films with a more modern look: ''Barbie in Rock 'N Royals'' in 2015 and ''[[Barbie: Princess Adventure]]'' in 2020.

The success of the first three films (''[[Barbie in the Nutcracker]]'', ''[[Barbie as Rapunzel]]'' and ''[[Barbie of Swan Lake]]'') led to the princess-themed lineup in the series, releasing its debut entirely original princess film ''[[Barbie and the Magic of Pegasus]]'' in 2005. The first original film in the franchise, based on a pre-existing toy line, ''[[Barbie: Fairytopia]]'', released earlier that year, spawned the first-in-series franchise made up of two [[sequel]]s (''[[Barbie: Fairytopia#Barbie Fairytopia: Mermaidia (2006)|Barbie Fairytopia: Mermaidia]]'' in 2006 and ''[[Barbie: Fairytopia#Barbie Fairytopia: Magic of the Rainbow (2007)|Barbie Fairytopia: Magic of the Rainbow]]'' in 2007) and two [[spin-off (media)|spin-offs]] (''[[Barbie: Fairytopia#Barbie: Mariposa (2008)|Barbie: Mariposa]]'' in 2008 and its sequel, ''[[Barbie: Fairytopia#Barbie: Mariposa & the Fairy Princess (2013)|Barbie: Mariposa & the Fairy Princess]]'', in 2013).''<ref>{{Cite web |last=Crombar |first=Brandon |date=27 February 2015 |title=All 37 Best Barbie Movies List In Order with Infographic (2020) |url=https://featuredanimation.com/barbie-movies/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200727002306/https://featuredanimation.com/barbie-movies/ |archive-date=27 July 2020 |access-date=26 July 2020 |website=Featured Animation |language=en-US}}</ref>''

Starting with ''[[Barbie in a Mermaid Tale]]'' in early 2010, the film series moved away from the classic princess and fairy stories to focus on more modern themes like fashion, music and on stories revolving around [[list of Barbie's friends and family|Barbie's friends, family]] and [[Barbie's careers|careers]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jane |first=Emma A. |year=2013 |title="No bos olib" – On the gynocentrism and sparkly separatism of the Barbie movies |url=https://fusion-journal.com/issue/002-fusion-the-limits-of-virtuality/no-bos-olib-on-the-gynocentrism-and-sparkly-separatism-of-the-barbie-movies/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200724030520/https://fusion-journal.com/issue/002-fusion-the-limits-of-virtuality/no-bos-olib-on-the-gynocentrism-and-sparkly-separatism-of-the-barbie-movies/ |archive-date=24 July 2020 |access-date=26 July 2020 |website=Fusion Journal}}</ref> ''Barbie: Video Game Hero'', the 35th film entry released in early 2017, marked the last time a Barbie film both aired on American television and released on home video formats.<ref>{{Cite web|title=List of movies and specials aired on Nickelodeon|website=Nickandmore!|url=https://www.nickandmore.com/kids-tv-history/list-of-movies-and-specials-aired-on-nickelodeon/|access-date=26 March 2022|language=en-US}}</ref> In 2017, the film series was put on hiatus after ''[[Barbie Dolphin Magic|Barbie: Dolphin Magic]]'', which served as the pilot to the inaugural [[television show]] in the franchise, ''[[Barbie Dreamhouse Adventures|Barbie: Dreamhouse Adventures]]'', in Mattel's attempt to focus on expanding the franchise to other audiovisual media formats. Mattel later revamped the films in 2020 as animated "specials", beginning with the musical, ''[[Barbie: Princess Adventure]]''.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie: Princess Adventures brings movie series back|url=http://www.toonbarn.com/other-cartoons/barbie-princess-adventures-brings-movie-series-back/|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200313042528/http://www.toonbarn.com/other-cartoons/barbie-princess-adventures-brings-movie-series-back/|archive-date=13 March 2020|website=ToonBarn|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=18 February 2021|last=Goldberg|first=Lesley|title=Mattel Sets Pair of Barbie Animated Specials (Exclusive)|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/mattel-sets-pair-of-barbie-animated-specials-exclusive-4134363/|website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|access-date=25 July 2022}}</ref>

==Animated specials==
Before the films and the video games, Mattel released two animated [[television special]]s in 1987. Another animated special was released on February 1, 2023, on YouTube to complement a doll line exclusively aimed at [[preschooler]]s.

* ''[[Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World]]'', a 1987 animated TV special created by [[DIC Animation City]] with [[Saban Entertainment|Saban Productions]] and featuring Barbie as the leader of a rock band. It is supposedly the pilot to a daily Barbie animated series that was scrapped in 1988.<ref name="Maxie's">{{cite web |last=Erickson |first=Hal |title=Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World (1987) |url=https://www.allmovie.com/movie/barbie-and-the-rockers-out-of-this-world-v3926 |website=[[AllMovie]] |quote=Originally distributed by DIC Enterprises in the fall of 1987, Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World was supposed to have been the pilot for a Monday-Friday Barbie cartoon series. Unfortunately, negotiations between DIC and Mattel fell through, and the project eventually emerged with a whole new set of characters—and the sponsorship of Mattel rival Hasbro—as Maxie's World in 1988. |access-date=13 December 2017}}</ref>
* ''[[Barbie and the Sensations: Rockin' Back to Earth]]'', a 1987 sequel to ''Barbie and the Rockers: Out of This World'', where Barbie and her band return from space only to end up in the 1950s.
* ''My First Barbie: Happy Dreamday,'' a 40-minute musical animated special produced by Canada-based Kickstart Entertainment and released on February 1, 2023, on YouTube, featuring Barbie and her friends as they prepare for a surprise party for Chelsea.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gates |first=Kaitlin |date=17 January 2023|title=New My First Barbie is the first designed for preschool-aged children |url=https://www.ksby.com/new-my-first-barbie-is-designed-for-preschoolers |access-date=3 February 2023|website=[[KSBY]] |quote=The launch of My First Barbie is accompanied by the first-ever Barbie content dedicated to preschool-aged children, titled "My First Barbie: Happy DreamDay," which will debut Jan. 31 on the Barbie YouTube channel. The animated special follows Barbie and her friends as they prepare for a surprise party. Storylines highlight preschool themes of friendship and family, with simplified plots, original music and life lessons. The four main dolls featured in the special are Barbie "Malibu" Roberts, Barbie "Brooklyn" Roberts, Teresa and Renee.}}</ref>

''[[Barbie: Life in the Dreamhouse]]'' (2012–2015) was the first ''Barbie'' animated series, airing exclusively on [[YouTube]] with 75 episodes. There were two TV specials which aired in the United States on [[Nickelodeon]].

Following the success of ''Barbie: Life in the Dreamhouse'', Mattel developed ''Barbie Vlogs'' (2015–2022), a YouTube-exclusive CGI-animated series featuring Barbie as a [[vlogger]] either by herself or along with one or more of [[list of Barbie's friends and family|her family and friends]].<ref>{{Cite web|date=19 June 2015|title=Barbie Vlogger|url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt9425096/|work=[[Mattel Television|Mattel Creations]]|publisher=[[Mattel]]|access-date=27 March 2022|via=[[IMDb]]}}</ref>

In 2015, the ''Barbie'' YouTube channel also released ''Barbie: Motion Comics.'' A web-based animated [[motion comic]] series composed of  three miniseries: ''Be Super'' (inspired by ''Barbie in Princess Power''), ''Raise Your Voice'' (inspired by ''Barbie in Rock 'N Royals''), and ''Puppy Adventures'' (inspired by ''Barbie & Her Sisters in The Great Puppy Adventure'').<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barbie: Motion Comics |url=https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL5BsRl9zFaeQ3ByCbqsDOHeEiabpNWxyL |access-date=28 July 2022 |work=[[Barbie]] |publisher=[[Mattel]] |via=[[YouTube]]}}</ref>

''[[Barbie Dreamtopia]]'' is a web-exclusive franchise that began in January 2016 with the release of 2-minute to 4-minute shorts on YouTube. ''Mattel'' then released a 44-minute [[television special]] (airing on television in 5 countries), one web special, and an initially-exclusive [[YouTube Kids]] series with 26 episodes. The latter was published on YouTube from November 5, 2017, to April 1, 2018, and aired as a traditional television series in over 6 countries.

''Barbie 3D Dreamworld'' is a YouTube-exclusive web series franchise created by [[Republic of Ireland|Ireland]]-based Relish Studios. The first episode was released on January 25, 2021. The series takes place within the doll world. Each season varies from 4-8 episodes in total, with each individual episode lasting around 3–4 minutes on average. It is composed of several different miniseries, which include: ''Barbie: Fashion Fun'', ''Barbie and the Nutcracker'', ''Barbie: Dream Magic'', ''Barbie: Camp Sister Switch'',  and ''Barbie: Return to Dreamtopia'', among others.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie's Dreamworld|url=https://reli.sh/animation/project/barbie/|website=Relish Studios|access-date=23 April 2022}}</ref>

''Barbie: Life in the City'' is a YouTube-exclusive web series that debuted on September 15, 2022, focusing on Barbie "Brooklyn" Roberts and expanding her character to emphasize her role and prominence within the brand.<ref name="Brooklyn Barbie">{{Cite web|last=Lash|first=Jolie|date=30 August 2022|title='Barbie Mermaid Power' Trailer Promises Underwater Fun & Friendship in Netflix Movie Musical (Exclusive Video)|url=https://www.thewrap.com/barbie-mermaid-power-trailer-netflix/|access-date=31 August 2022|website=[[TheWrap]]|quote=Among them are the upcoming YouTube series "Barbie: Life in the City," TheWrap can exclusively reveal. A third project in the works is "Barbie: It Takes Two Part 2," which TheWrap can reveal will premiere Oct. 1 on Netflix. It will once again follow Brooklyn and Malibu Barbies as they engage in "city hijinks and aspirations of musical stardom."}}</ref>

==Cancelled projects==
In an earlier version of the first ''Toy Story'' film, Barbie was supposed to have a pivotal role. However, Mattel did not authorize her use to [[Pixar]], as they feared the film would not be successful. Additionally, they wanted to maintain Barbie's neutrality wherein every girl who bought a Barbie doll could imagine her as they wanted, rather than as she was portrayed in a particular film. After the success of the first film, multiple Barbie dolls had small roles in ''Toy Story 2'', before the debut of the franchise's definitive iteration in [[Toy Story 3|the second sequel]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Garber|first=Megan|date=30 June 2014|title=Toy Story Was Originally Going to Feature G.I. Joe and Barbie|url=https://www.theatlantic.com/entertainment/archive/2014/06/toy-story-was-originally-going-to-star-gi-joe-and-barbie/373675/|website=[[The Atlantic]]|access-date=9 March 2023}}</ref>

The 1999 short film commercial, ''Barbie as Sleeping Beauty,'' which was released on [[Nickelodeon]] as a tie-in to a doll line and video game of the same name, was originally pitched to be a full-length film. At the peak of classic princess-themed films in the 2000s, Mattel continued to pitch the idea of adapting [[Sleeping Beauty|the fairy tale]] based on the [[The Sleeping Beauty (ballet)|ballet]], completing the adaptations of all three [[Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky|Tchaikovsky]]'s ballets into the franchise. However, it was scrapped without any disclosure on the status of its development while doll lines were released on multiple occasions.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Mattel Barbie as Sleeping Beauty Products|url=https://service.mattel.com/us/results.aspx?Ntt=sleeping%20beauty%20barbie|website=Mattel Service|access-date=9 March 2023}}</ref> Although the reason behind the cancellation is closely linked to [[The Walt Disney Company]] being granted to trademark the name "Princess Aurora" and included the acquisition of some of the music (also based on the ballet) for its film.<ref>{{cite web|last=Finke|first=Nikki|date=1 May 2009|title=An Attempt To Stop The Disney Machine|url=https://deadline.com/2009/05/an-attempt-to-stop-the-disney-machine-9137/|website=[[Deadline Hollywood]]|access-date=25 April 2016}}</ref><ref name="DisSB" />

=== Accolades ===
{{Main|List of accolades received by Barbie (film)}}

Barbie franchise has received multiple performing arts awards. ''Barbie in the 12 Dancing Princesses''<ref>{{Cite web |author=Variety Staff |date=2007-03-14 |title=34th Annual Daytime Emmy Awards network totals |url=https://variety.com/2007/film/awards/34th-annual-daytime-emmy-awards-network-totals-1117961131/ |access-date=2024-02-15 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> and ''Barbie Fairytopia: Magic of the Rainbow''<ref>{{Cite web |author=Variety Staff |date=2008-04-30 |title=Daytime Emmys nominations |url=https://variety.com/2008/film/awards/daytime-emmys-nominations-1117984862/ |access-date=2024-02-15 |website=Variety |language=en-US}}</ref> earned nominations for [[Daytime Emmy Awards]]. ''Barbie the Album'' received twelve [[66th Annual Grammy Awards|Grammy Award]] nominations and won three awards,<ref>{{Cite web |title=SZA's Massive Year Continues, 'Barbie' Dominates & Big Firsts From The 2024 GRAMMYs Nominations {{!}} GRAMMY.com |url=https://grammy.com/news/2024-grammys-nominees-takeaways-most-nominations-firsts |access-date=2024-02-15 |website=grammy.com}}</ref> including [[Grammy Award for Song of the Year|Song of the Year]], [[Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media|Best Song Written for Visual Media]] (for [[What Was I Made For?|"What Was I Made For"]]) and [[Grammy Award for Best Compilation Soundtrack for Visual Media|Best Compilation Soundtrack for Visual Media]]. The film also received eight [[96th Academy Awards|Academy Award]] nominations, including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]], [[Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay|Best Adapted Screenplay]], [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]] (Gosling), [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress|Best Supporting Actress]] (Ferrera) and two nominations for [[Academy Award for Best Original Song|Best Original Song]] (for "I'm Just Ken" and "What Was I Made For"). It eventually won Best Original Song (for "What Was I Made For").<ref>{{Cite web |title=2024 {{!}} Oscars.org {{!}} Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences |url=https://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2024 |access-date=2024-03-12 |website=www.oscars.org |date=23 January 2024 |language=en}}</ref>

==In other media==

===Video games===
{{Main|List of Barbie video games}}

Mattel has produced dozens of Barbie [[video game]]s since the 1980s. These games are often tie-ins with other titles within the franchise. Since 2018, video games based on Barbie media are produced by Budge Studios.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Game Franchises - Barbie - GameFAQs |url=https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/games/franchise/741-barbie|website=[[GameSpot]]|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>

===Books===
Mattel has produced more than 400 books published by multiple authors and publishers based on Barbie since the early 1990s. Every film in the franchise has an eponymous book adaptation.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie|url=https://www.fictiondb.com/series/barbie~21072.htm|website=Fiction Database|access-date=26 July 2022}}</ref>

===Comic books===
Mattel has produced a series of [[comic book]]s about Barbie published by [[Dell Comics]] between 1962 and 1963 and [[Marvel Comics]] between 1991 and 1996. Since 2016, [[Papercutz (publisher)|Papercutz]] is currently the publisher of [[graphic novel]]s based on Barbie which are usually tie-ins with other titles within the franchise.<ref name="PCutz" />

* ''Barbie and Ken'', a 5-issue comic series published by Dell Comics from May 1962 to November 1963.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barbie and Ken Comics Values|url=https://gocollect.com/comics/barbie-and-ken|website=GoCollect|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>
* ''Barbie'', a 63-issue comic series published by Marvel Comics from January 1991 to March 1996.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie Comics Values|url=https://gocollect.com/comics/barbie|website=GoCollect|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>
* ''Barbie: Fashion'', a 53-issue comic series published by Marvel Comics from January 1991 to May 1995.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie Fashion Comics Values|url=https://gocollect.com/comics/barbie-fashion|website=GoCollect|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>
* ''Barbie: Halloween Special'', a 2-issue comic series published by Marvel Comics in October 1993.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barbie Halloween Special (Volume) |url=https://comicvine.gamespot.com/barbie-halloween-special/4050-35239/ |access-date=27 July 2022|work=Comic Vine|publisher=[[GameSpot]]}}</ref>
* ''Barbie and Baby Sister Kelly'', a special one-shot published by Marvel Comics in October 1995.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Barbie and Baby Sister Kelly (Volume) |url=https://comicvine.gamespot.com/barbie-and-baby-sister-kelly/4050-79404/|work=Comic Vine|publisher=[[GameSpot]]|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>

===Albums===
Mattel has released dozens of [[soundtrack album]]s and [[compilation album]]s based on Barbie since the early 1960s. Most of the films, especially those under the [[Musical film|musical]] genre, are accompanied by their eponymous soundtracks.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barbie Albums and Discography |url=https://www.allmusic.com/artist/barbie-mn0000788392/discography|website=[[AllMusic]]|access-date=27 July 2022}}</ref>

*''Barbie Sings!'', a 7" vinyl box set released in 1961 with the voices of [[Charlotte Austin]] and Bill Cunningham as "Barbie" and "Ken", respectively.<ref>{{Citation |title=Barbie And Ken - Barbie Sings! |date=1961 |url=https://www.discogs.com/release/1464416-Barbie-10-And-Ken-Barbie-Sings |access-date=2025-09-24 |language=en}}</ref>
* ''Barbie Sings! The Princess Movie Collection'', a soundtrack album released on October 5, 2004, that features music from the first four film entries in the series: ''Barbie in the Nutcracker, Barbie as Rapunzel, Barbie of Swan Lake'' and ''Barbie as the Princess and the Pauper.''<ref>{{Citation |title=Barbie - Barbie Sings!: The Princess Movie Song Collection Album Reviews, Songs & More |website=[[AllMusic]] |url=https://www.allmusic.com/album/barbie-sings!-the-princess-movie-song-collection-mw0000259947 |language=en |access-date=2022-07-27}}</ref>
* ''Sing Along with Barbie,'' a direct-to-video released on November 9, 2009, and a compilation of twelve songs from different Barbie films released at that time that its viewers can sing-along to.<ref>{{Citation |title=Sing Along with Barbie |date=2010-01-05 |url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt2862486/ |type=Short, Family |publisher=Mattel |access-date=2022-07-26}}</ref>
